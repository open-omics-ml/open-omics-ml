# Open Omics ML — Code of Conduct

## What this project is

Open Omics ML is a scientific community project that applies modern machine learning methods to published omics datasets. Our primary aim is to ask what robust ML can tell us about the biology — and to make that process open, reproducible, and collaborative.

Reanalysis is the method, not the message. We follow in the tradition of the [ReproHack community](https://www.reprohack.org/), who have established that reproduction and reanalysis are "beneficial scientific activity in itself, with useful outcomes for authors and valuable learning experiences for participants and the research community as a whole." We endorse this view completely.

This project is pre-registered on OSF [DOI: to be added], meaning our methods and research questions were fixed before any reanalyses were conducted. Findings reflect the pre-registered framework, not post-hoc judgement.

## Who this is for

Open Omics ML is designed for three kinds of participants:

**Students** — undergraduate and postgraduate students conducting reanalyses as research projects. This is a genuine learning experience in applied ML and open science, producing real scientific outputs and co-authorship opportunities.

**New lab members and collaborators** — researchers getting up to speed with a dataset, method, or analysis approach. Reanalysing a published study is an excellent way to develop skills in a real scientific context.

**Researchers seeking feedback** — researchers who want community feedback on an analysis approach before committing to a full study. Submitting a pre-publication analysis to the Open Omics ML framework is a way to stress-test your methods in an open, constructive environment.

## What reanalysis is — and is not

Reanalysis within this project means applying current best-practice ML methods to a published dataset to ask what the data can tell us with more rigorous methodology. It is:

- A scientific contribution in its own right
- A learning experience for the analyst
- An opportunity to advance the biological question the original paper asked
- A collaborative extension of prior work

It is **not**:

- An audit, investigation, or criticism of the original authors
- A judgement on the quality or integrity of prior research
- An attempt to discredit findings

The studies we reanalyse asked important biological questions and advanced the field. Machine learning best practices in omics have evolved rapidly, and studies that applied methods that were standard at the time are not being held to an unfair retrospective standard. We are building on prior work, not tearing it down.

## Expectations for all participants

**Frame findings as scientific results, not verdicts.** Report what the data show with more rigorous methods. Do not frame findings as "the original paper was wrong."

**Be specific and evidence-based.** All methodological observations must be supported by specific evidence — a quote from the methods section, a line of code, a figure. Vague criticism is not acceptable.

**Acknowledge context.** ML best practices in omics have evolved rapidly. Note when a methodological approach was common at the time of publication.

**Keep it about methods, not people.** No personal criticism of authors, implied or explicit.

**Use constructive language throughout.** Before submitting a report, apply the test in the student guide: could this sentence appear in a collaborative methods paper co-authored with the original authors? If not, revise it.

**Notify authors where possible.** Where a reanalysis produces findings that materially qualify the original conclusions, we strongly encourage contacting the original authors before public release, inviting them to respond or collaborate. This is standard practice in the replication literature and consistently improves both the science and the reception of findings.

## Authorship and credit

Students and contributors who complete a reanalysis following the Open Omics ML framework are credited in the project dashboard and, where their work contributes to the collective meta-analysis, as co-authors on resulting publications.

Contributors who suggest improvements to the pipeline toolkit or framework documentation are acknowledged in the project documentation and publications.

## Unacceptable conduct

The following will result in contributions not being accepted and, where necessary, removal from the project:

- Language that is accusatory, dismissive, or personalised toward original authors
- Misrepresentation of original findings
- Selective reporting designed to maximise apparent failures
- Harassment of any kind toward authors, contributors, or other participants

## Enforcement

Concerns about conduct should be raised with the project maintainer [contact to be added]. Reports will be handled confidentially.
